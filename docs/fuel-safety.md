# Fuel Safety Guide

Last updated: June 2, 2026
Owner: Walt Brannigan, Head of Repairs

Lantern Supply Co. has sold fuel lanterns since 1987, and in that time the same handful of
mistakes have caused nearly every accident we have heard about: the wrong fuel, refuelling a hot
lantern, and fuel stored where it should not be. This guide covers what to burn in each product,
how to store and handle fuel, and what to do when something goes wrong. It applies to the
Northstar Dual-Fuel, the Hagerstown Kerosene Lantern and the Heritage No. 7 oil lamp. LED and
solar lanterns do not use fuel and are not covered here.

If you are ever unsure, do not light the lantern. Call us at (301) 555-0147 or email
help@lanternsupply.example and we will talk you through it.

## Approved fuels by product

Each fuel lantern is designed for a particular fuel, and the fuels are not interchangeable.
Using the wrong fuel is the most common cause of damage we see at the Repair Center, and
damage from an unapproved fuel is not covered by the warranty.

| Product | Approved fuel | Never use |
|---|---|---|
| Northstar Dual-Fuel (LS-ND2) | Clearburn white gas or unleaded gasoline | Kerosene, lamp oil |
| Hagerstown Kerosene Lantern (LS-HK12) | K-1 kerosene or Clearburn lamp oil | White gas, gasoline |
| Heritage No. 7 Brass Oil Lamp (LS-H7) | Clearburn lamp oil only | Kerosene, white gas |

The Northstar is called dual-fuel because it burns either Clearburn white gas or ordinary
unleaded gasoline from the pump. White gas burns cleaner and keeps the generator clear for
longer; unleaded is a fallback for when you cannot get white gas, not an everyday fuel. It must
never be fed kerosene. Kerosene will not vaporise in the Northstar's generator; it varnishes the
tube and sticks the check valve, and the lantern will need a paid repair.

The Hagerstown Kerosene Lantern is a wick lantern and burns K-1 kerosene, which is the clear,
low-sulphur grade sold for heaters, or Clearburn lamp oil. Lamp oil burns with less smell and
is the better choice indoors. Do not use white gas or gasoline in any wick lantern; both are
far too volatile for an open wick and the flame can run back into the tank.

The Heritage No. 7 burns Clearburn lamp oil only. Kerosene will work mechanically, but it
smokes, smells and stains the brass gallery, and scented or coloured decorative oils leave a
residue that clogs the wick. The 10-year Heritage Promise warranty does not cover damage from
any fuel other than lamp oil.

## The fuels we sell

Clearburn White Gas (LS-FUEL1) is sold in 1-quart cans at $12.99. It is a naphtha fuel, highly
refined, with no additives, and it is what the Northstar was designed around. Clearburn Lamp
Oil (LS-OIL1) is sold in 1-quart bottles at $9.99; it is a clear paraffin oil for wick lanterns
and lamps. We do not sell kerosene. K-1 kerosene is available at most hardware stores and some
fuel stations; buy it in a container marked for kerosene, never in a gasoline can.

Shelf life of Clearburn white gas is 5 years in a sealed can and about 1 year once the can has
been opened; write the date you opened it on the can with a marker. Lamp oil keeps indefinitely
if the bottle is closed. Old white gas does not become dangerous, but it gums the generator and
the lantern will burn poorly. If a can has been open for more than a year, dispose of it as
described below and buy fresh.

## Storage

Store fuel in its original container with the cap tight. Do not pour fuel into bottles or jars
that are not marked for it; a clear bottle of white gas looks like water and has been drunk by
children. Keep fuel in a cool, ventilated place away from heat, flame, pilot lights and direct
sun, and out of the living space of the home: a detached shed or a garage cabinet is right, a
kitchen cupboard or a closet under the stairs is not.

Most Maryland jurisdictions limit the fuel a household may keep to 5 gallons, and some limit it
further; check your county's fire code. For most customers one or two quarts at a time is
plenty. The showrooms keep no more than 2 quarts on the floor as display stock, and the rest is
in the fuel room in Hagerstown; we hold ourselves to the same rules we ask of you.

Never store a fuel lantern with fuel in it for more than a few weeks. Empty the tank into the
original container (the Northstar's tank drains through the filler), wipe the lantern down and
store it with the cap off so the tank can air.

## Refuelling

Never refuel a hot lantern. Turn it off, let it cool for at least 10 minutes, and touch the tank
with the back of your hand before you open the cap. A hot tank holds fuel vapour under pressure
and will spray fuel at you when the cap comes off; this is how most burns we hear about happen.

Refuel outdoors or in a well-ventilated space, away from any flame, on a surface you do not mind
getting fuel on. Use a funnel. Fill the Northstar to the line inside the filler, not to the top;
the tank needs air space to build pressure. Fill the Hagerstown to no more than the bottom of
the filler neck, and the Heritage to the line on the font. Wipe up any spill before you light
the lantern, and move the lantern at least a metre from where you filled it.

Put the fuel container's cap back on and put the container away before you light the lantern.
An open can of white gas next to a lit lantern is the one picture we never want to see.

## Lighting and ventilation

Light fuel lanterns outdoors, in a tent vestibule with the door open, or in a room with a window
open. Every fuel lantern produces carbon monoxide, and the Northstar produces enough that it
must never be used in a closed tent, a camper, a vehicle or a small room. The Hagerstown and the
Heritage produce less but are not exempt. If you use a fuel lamp indoors regularly, fit a carbon
monoxide alarm in that room.

Light the Northstar with the control valve barely open and a match or lighter held at the
mantle from below; open the valve once the mantles glow. If the lantern flares, close the valve
and wait for the flame to settle before trying again. Light wick lanterns with the wick turned
low and raise it slowly; a wick turned up too fast smokes and the flame can jump.

Keep lanterns on a flat, stable surface or hung from a hook rated for the weight. The Northstar
weighs 2.4 kg full and is top-heavy. Keep it a metre from tent walls, bedding and anything that
can burn, and keep children and pets away from it; the globe is hot enough to burn skin for
20 minutes after the lantern is turned off.

## After a spill

1. Move any flame away from the spill, or move the spill away from the flame. Put out the
   lantern if it is lit.
2. Do not use a cloth that goes back in the house. Soak up the fuel with paper towels, cat
   litter or sand, and put the soaked material in a metal container outside.
3. Let the area air out. White gas evaporates in minutes; kerosene and lamp oil take longer and
   leave a residue that you should wash with soapy water.
4. If fuel is on your skin, wash with soap and water. If it is in your eyes, rinse with water for
   15 minutes and call a doctor. If fuel is on clothing, take the clothing off outdoors and let
   it air before washing it; do not put fuel-soaked clothing in a dryer.
5. If fuel is swallowed, do not induce vomiting; call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222.

## Disposal

Never pour fuel down a drain, onto the ground, or into household trash. White gas and kerosene
are household hazardous waste. Every Maryland county runs household hazardous waste collection,
either at a permanent site or on scheduled days; Frederick County's site is at the Public Safety
Training Facility and takes fuel on the first and third Saturdays of the month. Take the fuel
in its original container.

Old lamp oil can be burned off in the lamp it was bought for if it is still clear. Oil that has
gone cloudy or has a sediment goes to hazardous waste collection with the rest. Empty cans and
bottles go in household recycling once they are dry and the cap is off.

If you bought the fuel from us and want it gone, bring the can to either showroom and we will
dispose of it; we do not charge for this.

## Shipping fuel

Fuel ships by ground only, as a limited-quantity hazardous material (ORM-D), from the
Hagerstown warehouse. It cannot ship expedited or overnight, cannot be delivered to a PO box,
and cannot be shipped to Alaska, Hawaii or Canada. An order that contains fuel ships as a whole
by ground; if you want the rest of your order faster, place the fuel on its own order. Fuel is
final sale once opened and cannot be returned by mail at all, opened or not; an unopened can
bought in error can be returned to either showroom for a refund.

Never ship a lantern with fuel in it, including for a warranty or repair return. Drain it,
let it air for a day, and pack it with the cap off. A lantern that arrives at Dock B with fuel
in it is drained before it is looked at, and we charge nothing for that, but the carrier can
refuse the box and the law is on their side.

## When to stop using a lantern

Stop using any fuel lantern and bring it to a showroom or contact us if the tank leaks or
weeps at a seam, if the Northstar will not hold pressure after the pump cup has been oiled, if
the flame burns yellow and sooty on the right fuel, if a wick lantern's flame keeps climbing
after the wick is turned down, or if you smell fuel when the lantern is not lit. Tanks are not
repaired; a lantern with a leaking tank is replaced under warranty if it is within the period,
and otherwise retired.
