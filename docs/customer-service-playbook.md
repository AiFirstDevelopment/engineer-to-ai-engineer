# Customer Service Playbook

Last updated: October 20, 2026
Owner: Tomas Reyes, Head of Customer Service

This playbook is how the support team at Lantern Supply Co. handles the questions that come in
every day. It does not replace the returns policy, the shipping policy or the warranty policy; it
tells you how to apply them, what you can approve on your own, and who to ask when you cannot.
Read it on your first day and again when it changes. Changes are announced in the support Slack
channel and the date at the top moves.

## Channels and hours

Support comes in by phone at (301) 555-0147, by email to help@lanternsupply.example, and by chat
on the online store. All three are handled from the Frederick office. Our hours are Monday through
Friday, 8:30 AM to 6:00 PM Eastern, and Saturday 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM Eastern. We are closed on
Sundays and on the six company holidays.

Every email is answered within 1 business day; aim for the same day. Phone callers wait less than
3 minutes; when the queue passes 3 minutes the shift lead takes calls too. Chat is answered within
3 minutes during support hours. Outside hours, chat shows a form that becomes an email.

All three channels log in Gorgias, our help desk. One customer, one conversation: if someone
emails and then calls, merge the tickets so the next person sees the whole story.

## Tone

Plain, warm and specific. Use the customer's name once. Say what you will do and when. Do not
apologize three times for one problem; apologize once and fix it. Never blame the carrier, the
warehouse or the customer. If the answer is no, say the policy and the reason, then say what you
can do instead. Most of our customers know lanterns well, so do not talk down to them, and do not
guess about a product; look it up or ask the Repair Center.

## Looking up an order

Every order has a number in the form LS-2026-xxxxxx: the letters LS, the year the order was
placed, and six digits. An example is LS-2026-004871. Customers find it in the confirmation email
and on the packing slip. Look orders up in the Hearth admin by number, by email address or by the
last four digits of the card.

If the customer does not have the number, ask for the email on the order and the approximate
date. Since Hearth 4.1 a customer can also look up their own order on the online store without an
account using the order number and the postal code on the shipping address.

Never read a full card number, a full address or the contents of an order to anyone who cannot
give you the order number or the email it was placed with.

## Returns

The return window is 40 days from the delivery date recorded by the carrier. It is not counted
from the order date and, since Hearth 4.1 on May 19, 2026, not from the ship date. Check the
delivery scan in the Hearth admin before you tell a customer whether they are inside the window.
Orders delivered between November 1 and December 24 may be returned until January 31.

Returned items must be unused, in the original packaging, with all accessories. Any fuel lantern
or oil lamp that has had fuel in it cannot be returned; those go to warranty if there is a defect.
Clearance items marked "Last Light", custom-engraved items, gift cards, opened fuel, mantles,
wicks and other consumables are final sale.

### Issuing an RA number

Every mailed return needs a return authorization number in the form RA-xxxxxx (six digits), for
example RA-118204. Customers get one themselves through Start a return on the online store, which
has issued RA numbers automatically since Hearth 4.1. On the phone, open the order in the Hearth
admin, choose Create return, pick the items and the reason, and read the RA number back to the
customer. Also email it. A return that arrives at Hagerstown without an RA number waits in the
"no RA" bin until someone matches it, which adds days to the refund, so always issue one.

In-store returns at either showroom do not need an RA number, only the packing slip or the order
number.

### Restocking fee and return shipping

A 12% restocking fee applies to opened, non-defective items. It is waived on exchanges and on
defective items. It is not charged on items returned unopened. Say the fee before you issue the RA
number; nobody should learn about it from the refund amount.

Return shipping is paid by the customer unless the item is defective or we shipped the wrong item.
We offer a prepaid return label for a flat $8.50, deducted from the refund. For a defective item
or our mistake, send the label at no charge and do not charge the restocking fee.

### Refunds

A refund goes to the original payment method within 7 business days of the return arriving at
the Hagerstown warehouse. Tell the customer the clock starts on arrival, not on the day they
ship, and that their bank may take a few more days to show it. Refunds over $500 need Tomas
Reyes's approval before they are released (see Goodwill and approvals).

## Damaged and lost packages

### Damaged on arrival

A customer who receives a damaged item has 5 days from delivery to report it, with photos of the
item and the box. Open a damage claim in the Hearth admin and attach the photos. Ship a
replacement right away. For items under $50 we do not ask for the damaged item back; tell the
customer to dispose of it or keep it. For items of $50 and over, send a prepaid label at no
charge and note on the ticket that the damaged item is expected.

If a fuel product arrives leaking, tell the customer not to open the box further, to put it
outside or in a garage, and to dispose of it through their county's household hazardous waste
collection. Do not ask them to ship fuel back. Replace it.

### Lost in transit

A package counts as lost when it has not arrived 10 business days after the expected delivery
date shown in tracking. Before that, ask the customer to check with neighbors and the carrier.
Once a package is lost, ship a replacement first and file the carrier claim afterwards; the
customer does not wait for the carrier. Note the carrier claim number on the ticket. If the
replacement is also fuel, confirm the address is not a PO box.

A package shown as delivered that the customer says did not arrive is handled the same way after
a 2-business-day wait, with one exception: for orders over $400, which require a signature, pull
the signature image from the carrier before replacing.

## Warranty claims

The standard warranty is 2 years from the purchase date and covers manufacturing defects. The
Sentinel 1000 and Sentinel 1000 Pro carry 5 years, the Heritage No. 7 carries 10 years,
rechargeable batteries and battery packs carry 1 year, and Second Light refurbished items carry
1 year. Mantles, globes, wicks, bulbs and fuel caps are consumables and are not covered. Drops,
water beyond the IP rating, unapproved fuel, modifications and commercial rental use are not
covered.

A customer starts a claim by emailing help@lanternsupply.example with the order number and
photos. Open a warranty claim in the Hearth admin; it issues a number in the form WC-xxxxxx, for
example WC-030517. The Repair Center decides within 5 business days whether to repair or replace,
and the choice is ours. For an approved claim, send the customer a prepaid label to the
Hagerstown Repair Center at no charge. A claim with no order number can be matched by email
address or by the serial number on the base of the lantern.

The warranty transfers with a gift but not with a resale. If a customer bought the item second
hand, explain this and offer a paid repair.

## Repairs

Any repair, under warranty or paid, gets a repair ticket in the form RT-xxxxx (five digits), for
example RT-20931. Create it in the Hearth admin from the warranty claim or from scratch for a paid
repair. The ticket number goes on the box and in the email to the customer.

Customers can drop a lantern off at either showroom or mail it to Lantern Supply Co. Repair
Center, 1180 Downsville Pike, Dock B, Hagerstown, MD 21740. Annapolis drop-offs are shipped to
Hagerstown weekly on Tuesdays, so tell Annapolis customers to add a week. Walt Brannigan, Head
of Repairs, emails a quote for paid repairs before any work is done; nothing is charged until the
customer approves the quote. Fuel lanterns must be emptied before they are mailed; tell the
customer, and put it in the email.

## Goodwill and approvals

You may give a goodwill credit of up to $25 per incident without approval: a discount code, a
free mantle pack, a refunded shipping charge. Log it on the ticket with a one-line reason.

Above $25, ask the shift lead. The Three Lights rule covers larger amounts: any refund, credit or
goodwill over $500 needs the approval of Tomas Reyes, and anything over $2,000 needs the approval
of Priya Whitcomb-Nair. "Any" means any: a refund that is simply the price of a returned Sentinel
1000 Pro and a Northstar together is over $500 and needs Tomas's sign-off before it is released,
even though the policy clearly allows it. Approvals are recorded on the ticket as a note from the
approver, not as a verbal okay.

## Escalation path

Agent, then shift lead, then Tomas Reyes. Escalate when a customer asks for a manager twice, when
a decision needs an approval you cannot give, when a customer mentions a lawyer, a regulator or
the press, or when you are not sure. Hand the ticket over with a two-line summary at the top: what
happened, what the customer wants. Do not promise a callback time for someone else; say that the
shift lead will be in touch by the end of the next business day.

Safety reports, meaning any claim that a product caught fire, leaked fuel, overheated or injured
someone, go to Tomas Reyes and Walt Brannigan the same day, whatever the amount, and the product is
recalled to Hagerstown for inspection at our expense.

## Chargebacks

When a card issuer sends a chargeback, Harold Nkemelu's office forwards it to support. Respond
within 7 days with the tracking record showing delivery (and the signature image for orders over
$400), the order confirmation, and a link to the relevant policy page. If the customer also has an
open ticket, pause any refund on that ticket; a refund and a chargeback on the same order means
paying twice. Record the chargeback case number on the ticket.

## Worked scenarios

### Scenario 1: a return just outside the window

On Monday, October 12, 2026, a customer emails about order LS-2026-008316, a Beacon 600 ordered
on August 20 and delivered on August 29. She wants to return it because she bought a Sentinel 1000
instead. Forty days from August 29 is October 8, so the window closed four days before she wrote.
The agent replies with RET-01, adjusted: the lantern is unused and boxed, so the agent offers,
with the shift lead's okay, an exchange toward the Sentinel 1000 as a one-time courtesy, with the
12% restocking fee waived because it is an exchange. The customer accepts. The agent issues
RA-121877, offers the $8.50 prepaid label, and notes the courtesy on the ticket. The exchange
difference is charged when the Beacon 600 arrives at Hagerstown.

### Scenario 2: a Sentinel 1000 Pro that stopped charging

On Thursday, September 3, 2026, a customer calls about a Sentinel 1000 Pro bought on June 14,
2025, order LS-2025-002210. It no longer charges from the wall or the solar top. The lantern is
inside its 5-year warranty, but the battery pack is inside its own 1-year battery warranty only
until June 14, 2026, which has passed. The agent opens warranty claim WC-030517 anyway, because
the fault may be the charging circuit rather than the pack, and explains that the Repair Center
will decide within 5 business days. On September 8 Walt Brannigan's team finds a failed charge
controller, which is a manufacturing defect covered by the 5-year warranty, and repairs it at no
charge under repair ticket RT-20931. The lantern ships back on September 11. Had the fault been
the pack alone, the agent would have quoted a paid pack replacement instead.
